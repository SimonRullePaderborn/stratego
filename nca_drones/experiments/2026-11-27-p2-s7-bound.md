# Experiment card: 2026-11-27-p2-s7-bound

## Before the run

* **Phase and gate it serves:** phase 2, step 7 of the chain in `PROGRAMME.md` section 12.4, and the iteration gate
  of section 14.1.
* **Hypothesis (one sentence):** bounding the state after each update, in place of normalising the rule's input,
  does not lower closed-loop success on the 2,000 never-posed tasks, compared with step 6.
* **What would support it:** the closed-loop rate of this run (bf16, its own 2,000 never-posed tasks) is not below
  the rate of step 6 by more than max(2 s, 2 points), with s the standard deviation of the three rates of step 0
  (rule of section 12.4).
* **What would refute it:** a rate further below. Then the run is looked at on trajectories before step 8 is built
  on it.
* **The one thing that changes** relative to step 6: `--update bound`. The measured rule normalises the state per
  voxel (zero mean, unit variance over channels) on its way into the convolution and adds the update to the raw
  state, which can therefore grow with every iteration. Now the state enters the convolution as it is, and after
  the update it is divided by max(1, its root-mean-square over channels): a state inside the unit ball is left
  alone, a larger one is brought back to it. Weights, widths and everything else are those of step 6; the volume is
  still regrown from zero at every step.
* **Why the goal needs it:** step 8 keeps the state from step to step, over several times more iterations than a
  volume grown once; the measured rule lost 5 and 18 points at twice its iterations.
* **Also reported, with no threshold in this card:** the iteration gate for this run and for step 6: closed-loop
  success on the 2,000 never-posed tasks with the number of iterations multiplied by 2 and by 4 at evaluation, as a
  paired difference to the trained number (the gate of section 14.1 asks that the lower 95% bound stays above
  -2 points); the largest root-mean-square of the state at the end of growth.
* **Verification done on the cluster's CPUs first:** `test_sim.py`, which now ends with `test_bound` (a small state
  is unchanged, a large one is scaled to 1, and after 60 iterations of a rule with large weights no voxel exceeds
  the bound), and a short smoke run on the GPU.
* **How it runs:** as step 6, from `~/wf/runs/p2s7` filled from the Mac:
  `bash cluster/jobs/p2_old_recipe.sh p2s7_bound_s0 0 0.5 --battery 1 --codes 1 --C 32 --hid 128 --threads 8 --sim wf --device cuda --closed_search 1 --update bound`.
  It starts when step 6 has left the GPU. If an earlier step is not kept, this run is void. (Added before the run:
  the PI allowed the node's idle GPUs on 27 November, so this run starts at once on GPU 4, after the same check and
  with the same guard, while step 6 is still running on GPU 1.)
* **Commit:** the commit that holds this card, named in `~/wf/runs/p2s7/COMMIT`.

## A first start that was stopped, and the repeat (written before the repeat)

The run `p2s7_bound_s0` from commit `ca39397` was started on GPU 4 and stopped after about a minute: its loss was
NaN from round 0. The bound was written as the state divided by its root-mean-square clamped at 1. At a state of
exactly zero the square root has an infinite derivative, the clamp passes a zero gradient, and their product is
NaN; a volume starts at zero and the rule's update layer starts at zero, so the first learning step already hit it.
The test of the bound had given that layer weights and so missed the case. The bound now takes the square root of
1 wherever the state is inside the unit ball, which is the same value with a finite gradient; the test covers a
freshly initialised rule; and the trainer stops on a loss that is not finite instead of training on it. The run is
repeated as `p2s7_bound_s0b` from `~/wf/runs/p2s7b`, at the commit that holds this paragraph. Hypothesis, rule and
the change under test are those written above.

## After the run

The run `p2s7_bound_s0b` ran on 27 November 2026 from commit `9c264c1` in `~/wf/runs/p2s7b`, container
`wf-p2s7b-s0`, GPU 4 only. Files: `results/p2/p2s7_bound_s0b.*`. The iteration gate was measured on 28 November
with the code of commit `1eb96e4` in `~/wf/runs/p2gate` (`cluster/jobs/p2_iteration_gate.sh`, container
`wf-p2gate-a`, GPU 6); files `results/p2/p2s7_bound_s0b.x*.json`, `.x*.out` and `.gate.out`.

* **Result:** closed loop on its 2,000 never-posed tasks: 1,884 = 94.2% (bf16; interval 93.1 to 95.1) and 1,888
  (fp32). Open loop: 1,832 = 91.6%. Test set: 194 of 200 closed, 188 open. Chosen checkpoint: round 900. Step 6:
  89.05%. A round took 7.47 s (step 6: 7.97 s); the run took 1.9 hours on one GPU.
  **The iteration gate is failed.** The same checkpoint on the same 2,000 tasks, growth with 2, 4 and 8 times the
  32 iterations, as a paired difference to 94.2% (bf16):

  | Iterations | Closed loop | Paired difference, points | 95% interval | Open loop | Lower bound above -2 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 64 (times 2) | 96.15% | +1.95 | +0.92 to +2.98 | 89.30% | yes |
  | 128 (times 4) | 41.55% | -52.65 | -54.94 to -50.36 | 28.10% | no |
  | 256 (times 8) | 4.15% | -90.05 | -91.37 to -88.73 | 2.40% | no |

  In fp32 the differences are +1.85, -52.15 and -90.45, so the collapse is not a matter of precision. On the test
  set at 128 iterations half the tasks succeed and 41.5% end by timeout (at 32 iterations: none). Step 6's
  checkpoint, without the bound, on its own tasks: +0.15, -5.00 and -12.30 points. The card asked for the gate at
  2 and 4 times; 8 was added because section 14.1 names it. The repeat of the run's own evaluation with the newer
  code changed no task.
  Not measured: the largest root-mean-square of the state at the end of growth. Under the bound it cannot exceed 1
  by construction, which `test_bound` checks, so the number would say nothing.
* **Trajectories looked at:** none; the step is kept by its rule. In the breakdown of the evaluation, 51 of the 116
  failed tasks have the recharger 9 or 10 cells from the seeker (104 of 129 and 56 of 82 solved; step 6 solved 62
  of 141 and 3 of 81).
* **Supported, refuted or inconclusive:** the card's hypothesis is supported: 5.15 points above step 6. The gate
  that the step was built for is failed, and by more than without the bound.
* **What was learned:** bounding the state costs no success at the trained number of iterations; the 5 points over
  step 6 are within what two single runs differ by, so no gain is claimed. But the bound does not make more
  iterations safe. A rule trained to be right after 32 iterations from zero is right at 64 and wrong at 128, where
  the unbounded rule of step 6 has lost only 5 points. What the bound limits is the size of the state, not where
  the rule takes it. Whether a rule holds under more iterations is decided by how it was trained: the checkpoint of
  step 8, trained with a kept state over up to 150 iterations, changes by less than a point at 8 times its
  iterations (card `2026-11-27-p2-s8-kept-state`).
* **Next step, and the card it becomes:** step 8, the state kept from step to step
