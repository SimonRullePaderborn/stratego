Wavefront Design v2

Specification, version 2 · space-time cellular automaton for multi-drone pursuit

Unified Wavefront Design

Version 2 rebuilds the design from first principles for the target the project now has: a 24×24 map with obstacles and roads, ten drones of three kinds, and an evasive target that stays hidden until a sensor finds it. One lattice, one local rule and tubes as the only output that steers remain. Most of what version 1 added around them is removed.

Status

Nothing in this document has been built or run. measured rests on the earlier 8×8 experiments. decided was fixed by the PI at kickoff. needs approval touches what the benchmark means, or departs from "general mechanisms only"; nothing that depends on such an item is built before the PI has decided it (§15). estimate is replaced by a cluster measurement before it is used.

Terms

Lattice
The grid of voxels with axes time, x and y: one slice per time step, one voxel per map cell.
Rule
The one local update that is applied to every voxel at every iteration.
Tube
For one drone, the marks in future slices that say where it will be. A tube seen as a path is a strand.
Front, dream
The present slice; the slices ahead of it, which the rule fills in.
Clamp
To overwrite the input channels of a slice with what is known. State and output are never written from outside.
Code
A one-hot vector of length 16 that a drone receives for one episode. It selects that drone's channel.
Greedy rule
The rule acting without exploration noise.
Strategy
The greedy rule plus one stream of exploration noise.
World
A seed for everything the team cannot know: where the target appears and how it breaks ties.
Clone
One strategy run in one world from a saved state.
Trunk, cut, leg
A training episode; a saved state just before a target is drawn; the stretch from a cut to the capture of that target or to the leg limit.
Screen, confirm, harvest
The three stages of search at a cut (§7).
Ghost drone
A drone with a code, a cell and a tube that the simulator ignores: it cannot sense, capture or be seen. It is used to audit credit.
Scripted teams
Two hand-written reference teams that exist only on the benchmark side (§3).
Free cell
Any cell that is not an obstacle. Road cells are free cells.

Rules

  • Moves. Stay, north, south, west, east. Everyone moves one cell per step decided. A move into a cell the kind may not enter becomes a stay.
  • Order within a step. (1) Drones and target choose from the same state. (2) All move at once. (3) Capture is tested. (4) Every battery loses one unit, hovering included; a drone on a recharger's cell is then refilled; a drone at zero is then removed for the rest of the episode needs approval. A capture on the last unit counts, and a drone that arrives on a recharger's cell with its last unit survives. (5) Sensing. (6) In training, a new target is drawn if there is none.
  • Capture. A capturer and the target are on the same cell after the move, or they exchanged cells during it decided. A capture radius exists as a setting and is zero decided. An evaluation episode ends at the capture.
  • Target. It flees from any drone it can see and wanders otherwise decided. Proposed details needs approval: it sees every drone, of any kind, within two cells, through obstacles. If it sees none, it stays or steps to a free neighbour, at random. Otherwise it takes the option, staying included, that maximises its distance to the nearest drone it sees, measured to where the drones stood before the move, ties at random.
  • Information. All drones feed one lattice. The map is known. The target is known only inside some drone's sensing radius decided. A sensed cell reports "target here" or "sensed, empty". All distances are Manhattan distances, and no drone's sensing is blocked by obstacles needs approval.
  • Reward. One per capture. Nothing else.

Sensing radii are chosen so that a capturer sees the target one cell before the target sees it, and an observer can shadow it from three to five cells away without making it flee. That is what gives scouting a value.

Maps

Roads are laid first: junctions on a jittered coarse lattice, joined by one-cell-wide segments; a spanning tree is kept and a share of the remaining segments is added back to make loops; dead-end spurs are attached. Obstacles fill part of the space between roads. Three generator families are used in training (city blocks with alleys, scattered walls, braided mazes). A fourth family and twenty hand-drawn maps are never trained on. Every map must have connected free space, connected roads, and a road within four steps of every free cell estimate.

In evaluation maps, a free cell with fewer than two free neighbours is turned into an obstacle, repeatedly until none is left, so that a dead end cannot do the capturing. Where such a cell is a road cell, a neighbouring obstacle is opened instead and the road stays. Training maps are left as generated needs approval.

Training and evaluation

  • Training. After a capture the next target appears on a random free cell with no drone on it. There is never more than one decided. Batteries and removed drones carry over until the episode is reset after 96 steps estimate. A target that is not captured within 48 steps moves to a new random cell needs approval. Maps, grid sides of 12, 16 and 24, and teams of 1 to 5 capturers, 0 to 3 rechargers and 0 to 2 observers are drawn uniformly, without stages needs approval. Drones start on random cells their kind may enter, with battery levels drawn uniformly between 2 units and full.
  • Headline evaluation. A 24×24 map never seen in training, with the full team of ten. Half of the episodes start with the team gathered within two cells of one road cell and with full batteries; half start as in training, spread out and with random battery levels. One target stands farther from every capturer than twice that capturer's initial charge, and for the capturer nearest to it the way around the obstacles is at least four cells longer than the straight distance. The episode lasts 128 steps estimate. The rule acts greedily: one lattice, no noise, no search. Success is a capture.

Controls that apply to a team are first run with the scripted teams, to show that the benchmark has the property, and later with every model that is reported. The benchmark is frozen at the end of phase 1. If a control later fails to behave as stated, the learner is never adjusted to pass it. A change of environment values then needs the PI's approval and becomes a new pre-registered version on which earlier results are reported again.

Pre-registration

Before the first training run on a benchmark version, the following go into git: the parameter file, the generator version, the seed blocks, the control thresholds, and hashes of the validation and test sets. Test: 500 new maps with 4 episodes each. Validation: 200 maps with 2 episodes each. One checkpoint per training seed, chosen on validation, is evaluated once on the test set. Intervals come from a bootstrap that resamples training seeds first and maps second.

The bar needs approval. Proposed: the lower end of the 95% interval is at least 80% for the mean over five training seeds, each trained from scratch, on the 2,000 test episodes from new maps of the training families, with the greedy readout. The held-out map family and the hand-drawn maps are reported beside it against a secondary bar of 60%. Phase 5 uses the point estimate on validation as a development gate; only phase 6 tests the bar.

§4The bitter-lesson contract

Sutton's bitter lesson says that methods which scale with computation, namely search and learning, end up ahead of methods that build in what their designers know. Version 1 turned that into five rules. Version 2 keeps them with one exception, one addition and one rule that had to be loosened.

  1. No domain concept in the learner. Distance, meeting, escort, pincer, sweep, low battery, sighting and role are not named anywhere in it. Check a text search of the learner's source. Exception each drone's battery level is an input decided.
  2. Every ability comes from search or learning, and has a compute dial (§11).
  3. More compute never hurts. More iterations, a longer window or more clones must not lower success. Check §10. One of the two 8×8 checkpoints fails this today measured.
  4. No shaped reward, no demonstration, no staged curriculum, no solution heuristic. Tasks are drawn from one declared distribution. Selection uses the return and nothing else. Loosened version 1 also excluded every exploration prior and every curriculum. Version 2 admits two things that wording excluded, and lists both for approval: exploration noise held over several steps, and a training mixture over grid sizes and team sizes (§12, §15).
  5. Every prior that remains is written down with the test that could remove it (§12).
  6. The benchmark may know what the learner may not needs approval. Shortest paths, connectivity checks and the scripted teams exist only in the generator and the evaluator. Nothing they compute reaches the learner as an input, as a filter on training tasks or as a training signal. The learner's code imports none of theirs.

What the learner is told about a drone: its cell, its kind as one of three fixed labels, its battery level, and a code that changes every episode. The kind label is inherited from the 8×8 design, where each type had its own channel. What a kind can do is learned.

What the learner takes from the simulator. For acting: the next observation, the reward, and the cell an action leads to. For search and credit, in training only needs approval: saving and restoring a state, drawing what is hidden afresh, replaying a leg with some drones acting at random, and running a leg with one drone's sensing withheld.

§5The lattice

The lattice has time as one of its three axes. It holds a window of slices around the present: 3 behind it, the present, and 16 ahead. Slices behind hold facts. Slices ahead hold the dream, in which each drone has a tube that marks where it will be. The present is the front between the two.

Map (input)2Obstacle, roadEvery slice

What the input holds. Clamping writes the input channels of the present slice after every step: which cells were sensed, the target if it was sensed, and each drone's kind, code and battery. When the window slides, that slice becomes a past slice and keeps what was written. Future slices hold the map and nothing else. At reset the past slices hold the map only. A battery level is given in steps divided by 24, so that the largest capacity reads 1; a recharger always reads 1. A drone that has been removed is no longer written, its tube is no longer read, and it has no loss term from then on. The lattice is not told that a capture happened or that a new target exists; it sees only what the sensors report.

Codes. At the start of an episode every drone receives its own one-hot vector of length 16, drawn at random. Its tube is the inner product of the output vector with that code, which is one of the sixteen output channels. Because the assignment is redrawn every episode, no channel can come to mean a fixed drone or role. A rule built from rectified units can gate one channel without touching the others. Codes drawn as random rotations are the alternative to test not yet tested.

c  = conv 3×3×3 ( X )                      once per step: the inputs do not change between iterations
h  = relu( conv 3×3×3 ( S ) + c )          zero padding; the only place neighbours are seen
S  ← bound( S + W3 · relu( W2 · h ) )      bound: divide by max(1, root-mean-square over the voxel's channels)
Y  = Wout · S                              the 16 tube channels, one voxel at a time

All weights are shared across voxels, slices and iterations. There are no coordinate channels, no pooling, no attention and no iteration counter. With 68 input channels, 32 state channels and 128 hidden units the rule has about 370,000 weights. The kernel sees one slice back and one ahead, so information flows from the present towards the future and from a future meeting back to the present. The state is kept in full precision and only the convolutions run in a reduced format.

Plans longer than the window. A tube shows the next 16 steps of a drone's route. Whatever lies further ahead is carried by the state, which spreads across the map at one cell per iteration: 48 cells at reset, then 4 per step, while drones move 1. The labels a tube is trained on are the cells the drone really occupied over the next 16 steps of a kept leg, whether or not the capture lies inside the window.

  • Drones are steered by tubes, which are positions in space-time, and never by a field of arrows. A drone moves to the reachable cell where its own tube is strongest in the next slice.
  • There is no order among drones. One step costs k_s iterations of the rule, whatever the size of the team.
  • The lattice keeps its state. A plan made at one step is still there at the next unless new facts contradict it not yet tested.
  • When evaluating there is one lattice, no noise and no search.
  • Commit groups, a dial that starts at one. To commit is to clamp a drone's chosen next cell into the next slice before other drones choose. With two groups, a random half of the drones chooses and commits, the rule runs once, and then the other half chooses. This is version 1's mechanism at a fraction of its cost, and it is switched on only if two drones of one kind keep ending up on one strand.

§7Search

The rule can only learn what search has found, so search is where compute goes. A trunk is one training episode. At every cut, many clones of the team are started from the same saved state, each with its own strategy and world, and three stages decide what is kept.

round
    draw 32 trunks from the training mixture                                   map, team, start cells, batteries
    for each trunk, until 96 steps have passed or no capturer is left
        cut: save simulator and lattice just before a target is drawn          at reset, after a capture or a relocation
        screen     M strategies in W1 worlds, closed loop                      the same worlds for all
                   V[σ] ← mean over worlds of γ^(steps to capture)             0 without a capture
        confirm    the best E strategies and the greedy one in W2 fresh worlds 
                   σ* ← the best of them, if it passes the test below          else store nothing
        harvest    σ* in W3 further fresh worlds                               only these give weights and credit
        continue the trunk from the end state of one clone                     see below
    learn from the harvested legs (§9)
  • A cut is taken only at a moment when nothing hidden has been decided yet, so that every world is equally possible from the team's point of view. No belief filter is written by hand, and no comparison ever uses the hidden position. A second cut at the first sighting, where only the target's tie-breaks remain unknown, is a dial not yet tested.
  • A clone runs until it captures, until 48 steps have passed (the leg limit H), until the trunk's 96th step, or until it has no capturer left.
  • Three sets of worlds, three jobs. Screening scores only choose what to look at again. A strategy is kept only on worlds it was not selected on, and it is imitated only in worlds that had no part in keeping it. With 256 equal strategies, 8 worlds and a capture rate near one half, the best one looks about 45 points better than it is estimate.
  • The confirmation test. For each of the W2 worlds, d is the value of the candidate minus the value of the greedy rule in that world. The candidate is kept only if the mean of d exceeds 2.2 times its standard error, which is the standard deviation of d divided by the square root of W2. For four candidates that is a false-pass rate of about 5% estimate. A standing audit sends identical strategies through all three stages; they must pass in at most 5% of cuts not yet tested.
  • Where the trunk continues. If a strategy was kept: from its end state in one harvest world drawn at random, and half of the time from the greedy clone in one confirmation world instead, so that the rule is also trained on states it reaches by itself. If nothing was kept: from the greedy clone in one confirmation world.
  • Exploration noise. Each strategy draws a temperature between 0.25 and 2, an excursion rate between 0 and 0.3, and a hold length between 1 and 4. At every step a drone either starts an excursion with that rate, which is one random action repeated for up to the hold length, or samples its action from its scores, standardised over its five options and divided by the temperature. Noise is independent between drones, and the rule never sees it, so what search finds is learned as a response to observations alone. The measured recipe contained such held actions measured; their separate effect was not measured, and version 1 had removed them needs approval. The 10 points that were measured belonged to noise shared between drones, which this design does not use. It relies on more clones: before training, 256 independent rollouts found a reward about as often as 64 with shared noise, 7.0% against 9.0% measured.
  • Batteries carry over from leg to leg. A leg that starts with low batteries cannot be won without a recharger, so recharge-needed legs arise without any task filter.
  • Only improvements are stored not yet tested. The greedy rule's own captures are not imitated; at the start they are accidents. This differs from the measured recipe, which kept the earliest rewarded rollout even when it was the greedy one. Storing greedy captures with their credit is a comparison arm, judged by how often legs that were solved 50 rounds earlier are still solved.
  • A dial that is off at the start: redrawing the noise of a random block of one to three drones in the best strategies and screening again. Its cost grows in proportion to the number of drones, where sampling all drones independently needs exponentially more rollouts.
  • Dropped: recombination of recorded action lists once anything is hidden, search trees, value estimates, backups, and any search at evaluation time.

Why an untrained team finds reward

An untrained rule has flat tubes, so drones follow their noise. Against a target that sees two cells, a lone drone cannot capture in the open, so first rewards come from two drones closing by chance and from pockets in the terrain. An untrained team also loses its capturers within one battery life, about 12 steps, so early trunks are one short leg long; second legs and legs that start with low batteries appear only once capturers have learned to be refilled. How often a first reward occurs is not known and is the first thing measured on the cluster, before any training (§13). The levers, in the order in which they are pulled:

  1. More clones. This is compute and nothing else.
  2. Smaller grids in the training mixture needs approval. With the full team of ten, a new target has a capturer and a second drone within three cells in about 35 to 45% of draws on 12×12, against 4 to 5% on 24×24; with a team of five or six it is about 15% on 12×12. These are hand estimates for drones placed uniformly on a map without obstacles estimate, and being near is not yet a capture. Training maps also keep the dead ends that evaluation maps lose (§3).
  3. A target that sometimes fails to move, as a declared setting of the environment needs approval. This would reopen the decision that the target is as fast as a drone, for training only.
  4. Targets with a shorter sight in training needs approval. This makes the task easier in the same way a larger capture radius would, so it comes last.

Evaluation always uses the full target. The long evaluation episode, with a recharge, an approach longer than one battery allows, a search and a closure from two sides, cannot be found by sampling in one piece, and training legs end after 48 steps. It has to be assembled by the rule from what short legs taught. Whether that happens is the main risk of the programme.

§8Credit

A capture says that a joint plan worked. It does not say whose behaviour made it work. Two tests answer that for the kept strategy, in each harvest world. Both count captures within the leg limit.

  • In a harvest world where the kept strategy did not capture, every share is zero. Such worlds stay in the mean.
  • The first test is the rule of the audited 8×8 run measured, extended from "one drone at random" to random orders. With several capturers, those whose moves mattered share the credit, and one that merely followed gets none. Lesioning one drone at a time in closed loop would give every capturer nothing, because another takes over.
  • The second test is the only way an observer can earn credit: in the first test the others repeat their recorded actions, and those actions already contain what the observer found. It becomes a weight in phase 4, where observers first appear, once it has passed the ghost audit there not yet tested.
  • The threshold. A mean share counts only if it exceeds a multiple of its standard error, taken over the harvest worlds. The multiple is set by the ghost audit: a ghost drone must clear it in at most 2% of legs. With 8 replays, 8 worlds and 2 orders, a threshold of one standard error would let a drone that did nothing through in about one leg in four estimate, so the multiple and the number of replays are expected to rise in phase 3.
  • Shares are never divided by a small value and never used per run. A positive weight for drones that did nothing would repeat the failure that was measured when irrelevant motion was imitated: the recharger learned to stand still measured.
  • A second audit. On teams of up to five, the sampled shares are compared with the exact ones, obtained by averaging over every order of the drones.
  • Comparison arm: closed-loop reruns with drones removed from the simulator, in a random order. One mechanism for both questions, at 9 lattice runs per world for a team of ten.

§9Learning

for each batch of 64 harvested legs, with iteration counts redrawn
    rebuild each lattice by replaying its trunk up to the cut     no gradient

        for every drone i and every j = 1 … 16 with t + j inside the leg

                                                         the cell of drone i at time t + j )
        after every second step: back-propagate through those two steps, update, detach
  • The weight w(ω, t) follows what the team could know needs approval. Two harvest worlds are indistinguishable at step t if the team's observations in them have been identical up to t. A dream read at step t in world ω is weighted by the mean return of the kept strategy over the harvest worlds that are indistinguishable from ω at t, and it is imitated in every one of them. While nothing has been sensed, all worlds look alike, so a way of searching is imitated also where it found nothing: it was right there too, and the target only happened to be far away. Once the observations differ, a world stands alone and its dreams are weighted by its own return, so a chase that failed is not imitated. The rule is stated on observations and names nothing in the task.
  • With everything visible, every world stands alone from the first step, and the rule reduces to imitating the kept strategy's captured legs. The measured system did the same, except that it also kept the greedy rollout when that was the earliest rewarded one.
  • Each drone has one softmax over the cells of a slice: a drone is in one place at a time. There is no shared target per kind, which could not tell two drones on one strand from two drones on two.
  • Except in the first steps of a trunk, the loss starts from a state that has already run for hundreds of iterations and carries no gradient. Training on its own long-run states is what the published recipes for "more iterations must not hurt" have in common; here it comes with the kept lattice. Iteration counts are redrawn for every replay (K0 from 32 to 128, k_s from 2 to 6), and Gaussian noise of standard deviation 0.02 is added to every state update during replay.
  • What is stored for a leg: the seeds of map, team and worlds, the trunk's actions up to the cut, the leg's actions in each harvest world, the codes, the credits and the weights. The learner runs the simulator again from these to regenerate the observations. Legs are used in the round that found them. Optimiser Adam, learning rate from 0.001 falling to 0.0002.
  • There is no forecast loss, and no gradient or selection signal ever comes from the hidden position of the target. The diagnostic readout of §10 is fitted afterwards on saved states, in a separate process whose code the learner does not import.

Cost model

All figures are hand arithmetic on a throughput that has not been measured estimate. The first act on the cluster is to measure it.

  • Window at 24×24: 20 slices × 576 cells = 11,520 voxels. One iteration costs 131,000 multiply-adds per voxel. The input convolution runs once per step and costs 1.8 iterations.
  • One clone-step: 5.8 iterations × 11,520 voxels = 67,000 voxel-iterations. Version 1, with 12 iterations on its 27-slice window and a slightly larger rule, needed about 207,000 in the same unit, three times more.
  • One cut: 32 × 4 + 5 × 16 + 8 = 216 clones of at most 48 steps: 10,400 clone-steps, or 6.9 × 108 voxel-iterations. Clones start from the trunk's lattice, so K0 is paid once per trunk.
  • One round: search at 64 cuts, 4.4 × 1010; the sensing test, 11 reruns in each of 8 harvest worlds for every kept strategy, up to 1.8 × 1010; rebuilding trunks and learning, about 0.3 × 1010. Together about 6 × 1010 voxel-iterations when half of the cuts keep a strategy. Version 1 at this scale and at its own dials: about 2 × 1012.
  • At an assumed 110 to 440 million voxel-iterations per second per H100 (the earlier estimate of 100 to 400 million, rescaled to this rule): two to nine minutes per round on one GPU, and 35 to 140 GPU-hours for a 900-round run at full scale. On seven H100s, the number assumed until the cluster is known, that is 5 to 20 hours. A 12×12 run costs about a quarter of that or less, a 16×16 run a little under half.
  • The 900 rounds are carried over from the 8×8 runs. Under a hidden target the improvement per round is smaller, and the number of rounds may be several times higher. Budgets are set again after the throughput benchmark and a 25-round take-off test.

§14Working on the cluster

No project code runs anywhere but on the H100 cluster and its CPUs decided. That covers training, evaluation, unit tests, shape checks, benchmarks, analysis scripts and the drawing of result figures. The laptop holds the git working copy and is where ssh and git commands are issued, by a session attached to that folder; it runs nothing else. The assistant's cloud container edits text, builds this document and reads results; it executes no project code.

  1. Code moves by git only. A change is committed and pushed. On the cluster the exact commit is checked out into the directory of the run that uses it, so a run can always be traced to its source.
  2. Verification comes first and also runs on the cluster. Every increment starts with the test suite on the cluster's CPUs: simulator rules against hand-worked cases, tensor shapes, replays that must reproduce recorded episodes exactly, the text search and import check of §10, and the controls of §10 on the scripted teams.
  3. Jobs never depend on the connection. They are submitted to the scheduler and outlive the ssh session. Each job writes a small status file every minute (time, round, metrics, restart count), appends to a log, saves a checkpoint every 5 to 10 minutes by writing and renaming, and resumes from the latest checkpoint when it is restarted.
  4. Reading results costs one ssh call. Status files, metric tables and text renderings of trajectories are read directly. Figures and trajectory files that should be kept are committed on the cluster and come back through git, which needs push rights there.
  5. Every experiment has a card in the repository before it starts: the hypothesis, what would count as support and as refutation, the commit, the configuration, and afterwards the result and the decision taken. One experiment is one committed configuration and at most a few hours on the available GPUs. Failure analysis looks at trajectories, not at one number.
  6. Search and learning are separate processes, one search worker per GPU and one learner. Workers read weights from a file the learner replaces atomically, and write harvested legs as small records (§9). This survives restarts and scales with the number of GPUs.

Two files for the first session exist and have not been executed anywhere: nca_drones/cluster/probe.sh reports what the cluster offers without changing anything, and nca_drones/cluster/bench_rule.py measures the rule's throughput in three numeric formats.
